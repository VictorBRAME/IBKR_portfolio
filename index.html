A portfolio built to be replicated

This portfolio is designed to be replicable by small investors.

Large fund managers hold many positions and often own very expensive shares, which makes replication impossible for individuals: with a small account, a single share can cost too much to respect the target weights. Nevertheless, to avoid paying excessive fees, a sufficient position size and low broker costs must be taken into account. An amount of 5,000 is therefore recommended, even though the portfolio can be replicated with much less.

The portfolio follows a value strategy, sometimes buying on exotic markets. A broker with wide geographic access, such as Interactive Brokers (IBKR), is therefore recommended to follow it.

Our vision

As of Nov 2, 2026

This portfolio is built to protect and grow capital in an inflationary environment. Energy has been one of the main drivers of inflation in recent years. When inflation is driven by energy, producers benefit directly from the rise in prices. This is why most of the portfolio is built around the energy sector.

A supply shock that is not over. The war in the Middle East has disrupted oil production and shipping on a scale not seen in decades. So far, the shortfall has been largely offset by coordinated releases from IEA members’ strategic reserves. These releases cannot continue indefinitely: members must hold emergency stocks equal to at least 90 days of net imports, and depleted reserves will eventually need to be refilled, adding to future demand. Meanwhile, shut-in production and damaged facilities will take time to restore. As this buffer runs down, we expect supply tightness to push energy prices, and inflation, higher.

Lost refining capacity is keeping product prices high. Military strikes have damaged refineries in the Gulf and in Russia. The loss of capacity has widened refining margins and lifted the price of finished products such as diesel, jet fuel and gasoline, which feed directly into consumer prices. Because repairs take time, we expect product prices to stay elevated for longer.

High public debt makes negative real rates likely. Government debt-to-GDP ratios continue to rise. We believe that, to keep this debt manageable, governments and central banks will tolerate inflation above government bond yields, which means negative real rates. This has happened before: after the Second World War, the United States and the United Kingdom reduced their debt burdens largely through decades of financial repression. In such an environment, cash and nominal bonds steadily lose purchasing power, while real assets and companies with pricing power can pass inflation through to their revenues.

Energy equities tend to benefit from supply-driven inflation. When inflation comes from an energy supply shock, energy companies are on the right side of it: their selling prices rise while much of their cost base is fixed, so margins expand. Energy equities outperformed during the 1970s oil shocks and were the best-performing sector of the S&P 500 in both 2021 and 2022. This relationship holds mainly when inflation is driven by supply.

A portfolio built for inflation. For these reasons, the portfolio is built for strong inflation-hedging exposure, with energy as its core.

Energy holdings selected on value criteria. Within the sector, holdings are selected using value criteria: low valuation multiples, strong free cash flow and solid balance sheets. By avoiding expensive growth stocks, the portfolio has no overlap with the S&P 500 or the MSCI World, which are heavily weighted toward them. It can therefore serve as a diversifier for broad index exposure.
